BlocksNet

Study prototype inspired by TetriNET, the 1997 multiplayer block-stacking classic. One shared game engine, three different phone UIs. Pick one to play against 1–5 bots — previews below are live autoplay.

AClassic Portrait

The original TetriNET window shrunk onto a phone: big field left, numbered opponent fields right, special bar and attack log underneath, and a button pad with target keys 1–6 and D.

  • Most recognisable to TetriNET veterans
  • Precise buttons, partyline chat sheet
  • Busiest screen; smallest own field
Play option A

BSwipe Focus

No on-screen buttons. Drag to move, tap to rotate, flick down to drop. Opponents in a strip on top; drag your special chip onto a player to fire it. Attacks appear as toasts.

  • Largest playfield, modern mobile feel
  • Drag-to-target makes specials discoverable
  • Gestures are less precise for fast play
Play option B

CLandscape Arcade

Hold the phone sideways like a handheld console: D-pad on the left thumb, A/B rotate on the right, your field and a 3×2 board of every opponent in the middle — tap a field to attack it.

  • All 6 fields readable at once
  • Two-thumb play, best for long sessions
  • Requires rotating the phone
Play option C

Features carried over from the original TetriNET

12 × 22 field, 7 tetrominoes in the classic blue / yellow / green / purple / red palette.
Up to 6 players, each with a slot number 1–6 used for targeting.
Classic line sending: clear 2 → 1 line, 3 → 2 lines, 4 → 4 lines to every opponent.
Specials appear on your field when you clear lines; clear the line holding one to bank it (max 18).
Attacks / defense log and partyline chat; winlist kept on the device.
Levels speed up gravity as you clear lines; eliminated fields fill with junk.
